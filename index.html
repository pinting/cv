I thrive in interdisciplinary environments, as my goal is to integrate different perspectives of existence. I believe that human development is driven more by emotion than by technology alone. I wish to believe in a society where people are not just building blocks, but individuals who actively take part in the system they form.

Born in 1996. My earliest exposure to technology was the single computer in our household without an internet connection. I spent my days playing video games and exploring the file system, clicking on every installed executable and daydreaming about a database engine that could link documents and render multimedia content seamlessly. During the summer of 2006, I stumbled upon the digital manual of the video game StarCraft written in HTML 3 and was astonished by the rendering capabilities of Internet Explorer 6. Despite having no knowledge of English and no formal training, I learned HTML by dissecting the source code of the manual.

Since that summer, my passion for learning has never faded. I have experimented with various technologies, including cross-platform desktop applications, web applications, embedded applications, video-game projects, natural language processing (NLP) applications and algorithmic challenges.

Nowadays I am focusing on mathematics, building bicycles and writing open-source software.

Besides working, I am a Linux and FOSS enthusiast, in love with literature, music, theater and cinema.

Open-Source

oraculum github.com/pinting/oraculum December 2025 –

An SQL incremental parser and enforcer which guarantees grammatical and semantical correctness for a subset of the language without any post-validation.

The parser is composed of the following layers: constants as token-level DFAs built by a single Aho-Corasick tree of the vocabulary; regular expressions as lazy DFAs over Brzozowski derivatives; exceptions of reserved keywords as differences of parallel DFAs; the grammar as an infinitely growing graph determinized on the fly as each DFA at acceptance expands into routes; and a semantic state machine which depends on a GF(2) boolean algebra solver and a graph-based table import and join solver for consistency enforcement (of fields, tables and their relations). The state machine supports query stacks, grouping, ordering, filtering and functions. The performance-heavy and language-independent operations are implemented in a Rust kernel and exposed to Python through PyO3.
